# A World of Any Size, Streamed Through the Ring

**Built 2026-09-23.** `tilescroll` — the port's `level2/arm6309/cmds/tilescroll.asm`,
its art and world generated by `software/tilescroll/bench/mktilescroll.py` (the cast by
`walkcast.py`), checked by `software/tilescroll/bench/run-tilescroll.sh`. ⭐ **Its actors
are the video card's sprite walker's** (§8). Superseded text is in
[`history.md`](history.md).

---

## 0. Summary — the verdict in one table

| | |
|---|---|
| the view | **640 × 480, `VMODE` 11** — and a VRAM byte is one dot and one line, so the pixels are **square**. Every other scene in this repository runs a mode where a byte is one dot wide and two lines tall |
| the world | **4096 × 2048 pixels**, a torus, held as a 128 × 64 map of 32 × 32 tiles — 8,192 bytes assembled into the module |
| what reaches the card | **147,456 bytes** of tile bank. Not a picture: `zelda` read 491,520 and `stardew` reads the same |
| the blind spot | the ring is 1024 × 512 and the view 640 × 480, so **384 × 512 of VRAM is never on screen** — and it moves with the camera. The bank lives there |
| the cost of travel | **~9.1 ms of copy engine per 32 pixels** horizontally (16 terrain blits + one 16 KB strip move), **~4.3 ms per 16 rows** vertically. At two pixels a frame that is 0.57 and 0.54 ms a frame |
| the gate | ⭐ **all 524,288 bytes of the ring against the invariant**, after 1,200 frames of an eight-leg path with diagonals in both directions on both axes. **0 differ** |
| the actors | ⭐ **seven kinds in five sizes, each animated its own way, every one restored, saved and drawn every frame by ONE store** to the sprite walker (§8). The default cast's walk is armed ahead and **starts on line 480, the blank's first**, ending by line 504 at the median and 517 at the worst: **0 torn lines in 1,201 frames**, with flyers drawn as high as line 0 |

⭐ **This is the other answer to the problem `zelda` solves by being a room
game** (`optimizations.md` §11.1). A room bounds the actor count because the
game controls what is on screen; this bounds nothing and scrolls anywhere, and
the two together are the whole space of 2D games this card can carry.

## 1. The one fact everything follows from

The ring is **1024 × 512** and the view is **640 × 480**. So at every instant
there is a **384 × 512 rectangle of VRAM the raster is not looking at** — and
its position is a function of `HSCROLL`, which is to say it *moves with the
camera*.

```
  ring columns, relative to the camera at HSCROLL = h

    0                             640    704              992   1024
    |------------ view ------------|-marg-|---- bank 9 ----|-marg-|
    \_____________________________________/                \______/
          terrain: world columns wx-32 .. wx+704             ... and the
                                                             other margin
```

Put the tile bank in the blind spot and it is hidden for free. Scroll
horizontally and it walks into view — **unless it walks out of the way at the
same rate**. That is the engine: the bank is nine 32-column strips that rotate
round the ring, and the strip they vacate is where the next column of world is
drawn.

⚠ **Vertically there is nothing to do**, and that asymmetry is what makes the
whole thing affordable: a strip spans all 512 ring rows, so moving `VSCROLL`
never exposes it. Only the *terrain* has to be streamed vertically, and it goes
into the 32 rows the view is not showing.

## 2. The two invariants

Everything in `tilescroll.asm` exists to keep one of these:

```
  terrain is valid for world column slots   hsl-1 .. hsl+21    (23 of 32)
  terrain is valid for world rows           vlo .. vlo+511     (vlo = (wy-16) & ~15)
```

and **ring slot of world slot S is `S & 31`, ring row of world row R is
`R & 511`** — the ring *is* the world, modulo its own size, so nothing
translates coordinates.

### 2.1 ⛔ Why 23 slots and not 21

The view touches 21 slots (640 columns straddling a 32-pixel grid needs
⌈640/32⌉+1). A margin slot at **each** end is what makes both directions work.

Without it the newly-freed strip is visible **the instant `HSCROLL` moves one
pixel**: the view is `[h, h+640)` and the strip starts at `h+640`. With it, the
slot the camera is about to need is already terrain, and the work to refill it
has a whole 32 pixels of travel to happen in.

⚠ **The margin has to be on both sides**, not just ahead: the camera can
reverse, and the slot behind is needed one pixel after it does.

### 2.2 The rotation, and the off-by-one that is the whole of it

`hsl` has **not** been updated when `HNew` runs, so:

| going | window becomes | added | dropped |
|---|---|---|---|
| right | `[hsl, hsl+22]` | `hsl+22` | `hsl-1` |
| left | `[hsl-2, hsl+20]` | `hsl-2` | `hsl+21` |

The strip sitting at the **added** slot's ring address moves to the
**dropped** one's, `colslt[]` follows it, and then the added slot is composed.

⛔ **Getting either end wrong puts two strips on one ring slot and loses a
third.** `checktilescroll.py` reports it as *"no strip at ring slot N"*; the screen
shows it as a column of tile bank walking through the world. Both ends were
wrong in the first cut.

### 2.3 Vertically, half a tile at a time

The view is 480 rows of 512, so **the 16 row-slots of a 32-pixel tile are all
of them** — there is no whole row-slot to spare, and the trick that works
horizontally does not exist here. The vertical axis streams in **16-row bands**
instead, which is what the 32 spare rows hold.

Going down, the window `[vlo, vlo+511]` becomes `[vlo+16, vlo+527]`: the band
of world rows `[vlo+512, vlo+528)` is added, and it lands on the ring rows the
dropped band `[vlo, vlo+16)` was using — which is what makes the ring a torus
rather than a buffer.

⚠ **`vlo` is a multiple of 16 and a tile is 32**, so composing a whole column
is in general **seventeen** copies and not sixteen — a partial piece at each
end. Getting that wrong leaves a 16-row seam of the wrong world at the top of
every new column, and only when the camera happens to be on an odd half-tile.

## 3. What it costs

| | bytes | copies | engine |
|---|---|---|---|
| a column, 32 px of horizontal travel | 16,384 terrain + 16,384 strip | 17 + 4 | **~9.1 ms** |
| a band, 16 rows of vertical travel | 11,776 | 23 | **~4.3 ms** |
| the startup `Build`, 23 whole columns | 376,832 | 368 | ~115 ms |

At two pixels a frame that is **0.57 ms a frame** horizontally and **0.54
vertically**, or 1.1 ms on a diagonal — of a 16.68 ms frame at `VMODE` 11's
59.94 Hz. `HJOBN` = 2 and `VJOBN` = 4 steps a frame finish each epoch with
frames to spare.

⚠ **A scene that scrolls faster has to raise them**, or the camera walks into
terrain that has not been drawn yet. The budget is the whole safety argument.

⭐ **The strip moves in four pieces, all in ONE frame.** 32 × 512 cannot be a
single copy — `CHEIGHT` is nine bits, so 512 rows do not fit in one rectangle —
and it must not be spread across frames either: §6.2 has what that cost.

### 3.1 Why 32 × 32 and not 16 × 16

Costed both. Per 32 pixels of travel, 32 × 32 needs 16 terrain blits plus one
strip move: 32,768 engine bytes and 21 register protocols. 16 × 16 halves the
bytes and doubles the blit count, and the per-copy protocol then dominates —
⚠ and that protocol turned out to be **232 µs and not 60** (§6.4), which makes
the case for the bigger tile stronger than the estimate did. Bigger tiles win until the
bank gets too small to hold the art — 144 slots at 32 × 32, of which the
tileset uses 61 and the actors 16.

## 4. The tileset, which is generated

`mktilescroll.py` draws it: four terrains, three of them **overlays** painted on to
grass with their edges eroded wherever the cardinal neighbour is something
else. A tile is therefore (terrain, 4-bit neighbour mask) — 16 a terrain, which
gives a rounded blob edge without the 256 cases a corner-indexed set needs.
Decorations (a tree, flowers, stones) are drawn **into** grass tiles rather
than given a terrain of their own.

⛔ **Forest and not rock, and the reason is the palette.** RGB332 keeps three
bits of red, three of green and **two** of blue, so there is no grey in it:
every stone colour tried quantised to mauve or khaki. A dark forest is three
bits of green against grass's four and reads at a glance.

⚠ **And the interior gets variants.** A region is almost all mask-15 tiles, so
one of them repeating on a 32-pixel lattice is what the eye finds first.

⛔ **The low bits of an LCG are not random** — `u32() % 16` cycles through
sixteen values in lockstep with whatever else draws from the generator, and
scattering the decorations that way put **one** tree on a screen where
seventeen were asked for, and seventeen flowers beside it. `Rnd.i()` takes the
top bits. `zelda`'s creatures stopped moving for the same reason five days
earlier (`optimizations.md` §11).

## 5. How it is checked

`run-tilescroll.sh`, ~12 min, and the gate is **the invariant, not a screenshot**.
`checktilescroll.py` walks the same path the program walks, works out where every
strip ended up, and compares **all 524,288 bytes** of the ring against what the
invariant says they must be.

⛔ **And two mutations, because a gate nothing can fail is not a gate:**

| | |
|---|---|
| `m1` | the strip is **not** moved, so the bank walks into the world |
| `m2` | the vertical band is **not** composed, so the rows arriving at the bottom of the view keep the world that was there 512 rows ago |

Both are required to be rejected.

⭐ **And two legs about the actors** (§8.4), read off `walk.txt`, a line a frame
the host emulator writes from its model of the walker:

| | |
|---|---|
| `m0` | the default cast: a walk every frame, every walk ended in the blank it started in, **0 torn lines** — and keyed draws in the top 64 lines in at least a tenth of the frames, as high as line 7 or above, so that "nothing tore" cannot mean "nothing was up there" |
| `s1` | ⛔ **thirteen creatures, required to tear**, and required to tear **only above line 64** — and its ring must still be exact, because a late copy is a tear on screen and never a wrong byte |

⚠ **The leak check is over the 21 slots the view can reach and not the whole
ring.** The bank's unused tile slots are zero and its sixteen actor shapes are
keyed, so **88,272 bytes of legitimate index 0** live there — a whole-ring leak
check reports all of them and reads exactly like a real defect.

### 5.1 ⚠ What the first runs cost, and none of it was the engine

| | |
|---|---|
| **LF instead of CR** | the shell's line terminator is carriage return. A script written with newlines is typed in full, echoed in full, and executed **not at all** — and what that looks like is a program that ran and printed nothing. Three runs and a trace went into it before the `cat -A` that showed it |
| **`mksddisk.sh` instead of `mksyscard.sh`** | the ROM carries no filesystem, so a plain data card is a machine that does not start: `RKBoot Krn tbn0` and stop. Every claim afterwards fails for a reason that has nothing to do with the thing under test. CLAUDE.md's table says this |
| **a relative `OUT=`** | the recipe's make cannot resolve it — *"No rule to make target `build/run/…/tbox.bin`"* |
| **the camera started at slot 30** | `LoadB` drops the bank where it belongs when `hsl` is 0. Starting anywhere else puts the terrain window over two strips that were just loaded, and `Build` composes straight over them |
| ⛔ **`pshs a,b` pushes A as the high byte** | `deca / clrb / pshs a,b` made the band's slot index **256 times** too big, so every one of its 23 steps addressed the same world column and the same ring slot. It assembles, it runs, and the screen looks almost right — the band is drawn, just always in the same place |

⭐ **The one that is worth the most** is the last: it was invisible on screen
and obvious to the byte-exact gate within one run. The other four were all
harness, and the byte-exact gate is what made them *distinguishable* from an
engine defect at all.

## 6. The actors

**Built 2026-09-23.** The hero holds the middle of the screen and creatures
wander the world around him. Three things are different from `zelda`'s actors,
and each follows from the camera being free. ⭐ Since 2026-09-28 the video
card's sprite walker draws them (§8); what follows is what is true of them
whoever draws them.

**6.1 Its position is in the world, not the ring.** The ring *is* the world
modulo 1024 × 512, so the ring address is `(WX & 1023, WY & 511)` and the
screen position is `(WX - wx)` and `(WY - wy)` modulo the world. An actor is
drawn only while it is **wholly** on screen — half off the left edge would put
it in the margin slot, which is the one the streamer is about to compose or
hand to a bank strip.

**6.2 ⛔ Its save-behind is a bank tile**, because there is nowhere else: every
other byte of the ring is on screen or about to be, and the bank is the only
thing that moves out of the way. The tileset uses 61 of the 144 slots and the
art 32 (columns 7 and 8), so column 6 is free: sixteen 32 × 32 save cells, one
for each of the walker's slots, and no actor is bigger than one.
⭐ `TileSrc` looks the position up **live**, so a strip that rotates between a
save and its restore carries the saved background with it.

⛔ **And that is why the scratch strip moves in ONE step.** Every actor is
saved every frame, so a strip moved in quarters over two frames would carry one
frame's saves in half of it and the next frame's in the other, and restore half
the cast out of backgrounds a frame old. `HStep` moves the scratch strip as
**two 256-row copies** (`CHEIGHT` is nine bits) in the frame its `colslt` entry
swaps — after that frame's walk, and before the next. It is ~4 ms of copy
engine once every 288 pixels of horizontal travel.

**6.3 ⛔ Three phases, and here that is not negotiable** — which the walker
does by construction: every restore, last slot first, then every save and draw. `zelda` interleaves a
creature's save with its draw, which shortens the window in which it is erased;
it is allowed to because its wander boxes are **disjoint by construction**.
Nothing is disjoint here. An interleaved save sees an actor that has already
been drawn and paints it back as a ghost when its owner moves off — 1,752
bytes of it, and the **art colours in the residue** are what named it.

**6.4 ⭐ The hero is slot 0**, so his save and draw are the walk's first after
the restores. He is never parked, and his record is written first in `WRec`.

### 6.5 Most of them are standing still (withdrawn 2026-09-28; see history.md)

### 6.6 ⚠ A diagonal is 1/√2 of an axis

A camera moving two pixels in *each* axis crosses the ground at 2.83 — **41 %
faster than a cardinal leg**. `PathTab` is in **8.8 pixels a frame** and a
diagonal leg is **362** rather than 512, which is 2/√2; the camera carries a
fraction (`cax`, `cay`) so the whole part can be added each frame and the rest
kept. The creatures do the same with a one-byte 0.8 fraction an axis: each
kind has its own step (`K.STP`, `$FF` is a pixel a frame) and a diagonal takes
it × 181/256.

⚠ **The model has to do it identically** or the byte-exact gate is measuring
its own arithmetic — `checktilescroll.py`'s `walk()` carries the same two
accumulators and takes the whole part the same way.

## 8. ⭐⭐ The sprite walker's actors

**Built 2026-09-28.** The video card's fifth CPLD, `v3walk`
(`hardware/video3/docs/plan.md` §6.4, `optimizations.md` §12), holds for each of
sixteen slots a size, a save cell, and a record in each of two stream banks —
where to draw and which of 32 shapes — and on **one store to `SWCMD`** does every
restore (last slot first) and every save and keyed draw (first slot first) with
no CPU at all. `tilescroll` gives it every actor. ⭐ [`walker.md`](walker.md) is the programmer's guide to the registers, the tables and the traps.

### 8.1 The cast

`walkcast.py` draws it; `mktilescroll.py` puts the 32 shapes in bank columns 7
and 8, each in the top-left of a 32 × 32 cell with the rest keyed, and emits
`KindTab`:

| kind | size | shapes | animation | moves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hero | 16 × 16 | 8 | a two-step walk in the camera's direction | with the camera, at the centre |
| slime | 16 × 16 | 3 | squash and stretch, bottom-anchored | ground, slowly |
| bird | 24 × 16 | 3 | wings up, level, down, level | ⭐ flies to the top 64 lines |
| butterfly | 12 × 12 | 3 | wings open, half, edge-on, half | ⭐ flies to the top 64 lines |
| firefly | 8 × 8 | 3 | a glow that swells and fades | ground |
| gem | 8 × 12 | 3 | a crystal turning on its axis | ground |
| bat | 16 × 8 | 3 | a fast flap | ⭐ flies to the top 64 lines |

Each creature's `A.AN` gains its kind's speed a frame and the top two bits are
the frame, so the slime's cycle is 43 frames and the bat's 12. ⭐ **A cycle is four
frames drawn from three shapes**: the fourth frame is the second (level, half, …), which
`walkcast.py` asserts rather than trusts, and `CastMv` plays 0, 1, 2, 1. That is 26
shapes of the walker's 32; 26–30 are empty cells and ⭐ **31 is the walker's null**. **They animate
while resting too**, which costs nothing: every actor is drawn every frame
anyway. `tilescroll MODE FRAMES N` takes N creatures (default 6, one of each
kind); creature *i* is kind `((i − 1) mod 6) + 1`.

### 8.2 One store, armed ahead of the blank

A frame is: the camera and the creatures' logic (CPU only — the walk runs under it),
`ScBlank` (wait for the walk, then the scroll registers and `FCnt`), the streaming,
`WFix`, `WRec`, and **`WArm`**, which arms the next walk to start by itself at the rise
of `VBLANK` (`walker.md` §4.2).

- ⭐ **The CPU is never in the blank.** The walk that draws frame *k* was armed during
  frame *k* − 1 and starts on line 480 exactly, whatever the CPU is doing; the program
  arrives at `ScBlank` after its logic and finds it running or done. `Move` computes the
  new camera and `TSMove` applies it after the walk, so a column the camera crossed is
  streamed the frame it is crossed, as before.
- ⭐ **The scroll registers are written after the walk, for the next frame.** `VSCROLL`
  loads only in the blank, so the pair written in `ScBlank` (from `scx`/`scy`, the camera
  `WArm` froze with the records) takes effect exactly with the walk that draws in it;
  `HSCROLL` is written in the same breath, so the picture moves as one.
- **The records are double-buffered, and sent with a `TFM`.** `WRec` builds the next
  frame's 3 × 7 bytes in `recs`, in RAM, then SELECTs the bank the running walk did not
  draw from and sends them with one `tfm x+,y` into `SWDAT`. The GO's `CB` names the
  bank; the restore reads the other.
- ⭐ **Every slot is walked every frame, and an actor not wholly on screen is a null.**
  Its record carries shape 31 (`WS.Null`), so its save, draw and — next frame — restore
  are skipped by the card (`walker.md` §3.4). A hidden creature costs 15 dots and the
  walk is as long as what is visible.
- **The first walk is `NR`** (nothing to put back), and the end of the run waits out the
  last armed walk and then does a **restore-only** walk, bounded by a poll of
  `VSTAT.WALK`, so the ring gate sees pure terrain.
- ⛔ **The contract** — `WMODE` not 11, `WADV` b2 clear, no copy running, and nothing
  but free registers touched while a GO is pending — is kept by a `V3Wait` and a
  `V3Mode #WM.Direct` in `WArm`, and the emulator reports any breach as a `FAIL`.

### 8.3 ⛔ The tables hold absolute ring addresses, and the bank rotates

The save cells live in bank column 6 and the shapes in 7 and 8, and every 32
pixels of horizontal travel one strip moves to another ring slot. `WFix` runs
after the streaming and before `WRec`, notices from `colslt` which columns
moved, and rewrites what pointed at them: every slot's `SAVE`, both shape
tables. A null record points nowhere, so nothing else needs fixing.
⭐ Strips only move after a walk (a card write waits for one), so a walk never
sees a table half true; and the scratch strip moves in one step (§6.2).
⭐ A draw that crosses ring column 1023 needs no split: the walker's column
counter wraps at ten bits.

### 8.4 ⭐⭐ Tearing, measured

The emulator applies the walker's copies a row at a time on the card's clock
(25 dots of loads a copy, 6.216 dots a byte, 17 a row), and counts a displayed
line as **torn** if the walk still owes a write to its ring row when the beam
starts it. `walk.txt` records that per frame, with the topmost line any keyed
draw landed on.

| | walk | lines | torn |
|---|---|---|---|
| **m0** — 6 creatures and the hero | 0.76 ms median, 1.18 ms worst | 480 → 504 (median), 517 (worst) | ⭐ **0 lines in 1,201 frames**, with keyed draws in the top 64 lines in 212 of them and as high as line 0 |
| **s1** — 13 creatures and the hero | overruns in 53 % of frames, by up to 29 lines | 480 → 527 (median), 29 of the next | ⛔ **82 lines in 18 frames — every one of them in lines 0–13** |

⭐ **m0 has eight lines of blank to spare in its worst frame, and twenty-one at the
median.** The armed GO buys four of them (an unarmed GO, written when the CPU sees the blank, starts on 484–485),
and null records the rest: a creature off screen no longer costs
its three copies. ⭐ And s1 is what an overrun looks like on this card: a band at the top
of the picture the height of the overrun, and nothing below it — never a wrong byte in
VRAM, because the ring gate passes on s1 too. It tore 4,916 lines in 428 frames before the armed GO and the null record; now the
walk starts on time and an off-screen creature is free, so it overruns less often and by
less. ⚠ The run does not separate the two changes' shares of that.
